### Design: why the new patterns match exactly the same

- **`starts_at`, `any()`/`all()` branch.** Old: after `any(` or `all(` at position q, it matches if some `test` t, either right at q or after a `(` or `,` at or after q on q's line plus white space, is not preceded by `not(`, is followed by white space and `,` or `)`, and has on its own line, after it, a `)` followed by `\s*\)\s*\]`. Whether such a `)` exists depends only on where t is, not on q or on other candidates: it is a `)` later on t's line, followed by text that may run on across line breaks, and an earlier t on a line has every such `)` a later one has. So among the candidates on q's line only the first needs checking. The new pattern commits to it: the lookahead finds the first candidate, trying q first and then each `(`/`,` in order through `??` and `.*?`, and the backreference consumes it. The old `\s*` after `(`/`,` could also reach a `test` across a line break. That happens for at most one candidate (the white space must run from the last non-blank `(`/`,` of q's line to the `test`), so it is a second alternative, tried on its own: `.*[(,][^\S\n]*\n\s*test(?=\s*[,)])`. White space always precedes it, so the `not(` lookbehind holds there and is left out (the fuzzer showed that mutant equivalent before it was dropped).
- **`guarded_lines`** (the guard calls it on one line at a time). Old: it matches if some `cfg(` (`cfg`, optional `_attr`, optional `!`, `(`, white space between) has `\btest\b` at or after its end, or `macro_rules!` names an assert macro. A later `cfg(` ends later and has less of the line after it, so the first one is the only one to check. The new pattern commits to it the same way. Both branches are anchored at the line's start, the `macro_rules!` branch through `.*`, so `re.search` tries a line once instead of at every position. That keeps ordinary lines about as cheap as before: over the 52,857 lines of the largest crates.io file, the old rule takes 0.029 s; the new one with only its `cfg(` branch anchored took 0.067 s; with both anchored, 0.035 s (`gl-variants.out`; an unrolled-loop form is faster still, 0.020 s, but harder to read). Every variant decides the same, and every one that commits to the first `cfg(` takes 0.9–1.6 ms on the crafted line where the old rule takes 8.2 s.
- **`attached_above`**: unchanged, linear (bench).

**Behaviour changes to expose:** none. The guard makes the same decision on every input tried; a decision only takes less time. On ordinary code the new rule's cost is within a small constant of the old: the slowest real file of 40,059 takes 0.066 s against 0.058 s (`corpus.out`).
